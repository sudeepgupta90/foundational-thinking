# Scholastic Disputation — Codified Principles

> "By doubting we come to inquiry; by inquiring we perceive the truth."
> — Peter Abelard, *Sic et Non*, prologue (c. 1120)

## What it is

A protocol for reaching a defensible decision on a contested question. Where the Socratic
elenchus is dissolvent — it takes false confidence apart — disputation is **constructive**:
it exists to produce a determination that has already survived the best case against it.

Its central and most counterintuitive property: **you must make your opponent's argument
for them, in its strongest form, before you are permitted to decide.** Not as courtesy.
As a structural precondition. In the *quaestio* form, the objections come *first*, and
they are the part the author does not believe.

## Provenance

The *disputatio* was the working method of the medieval universities — Paris, Oxford,
Bologna — from roughly the twelfth through the fifteenth century. It grew out of Abelard's
*Sic et Non*, which assembled 158 theological questions with authoritative sources
contradicting each other on each, and declined to resolve them: the contradiction was
presented as the *starting point* of inquiry rather than an embarrassment to be hidden.

Its mature literary form is the *quaestio* of Thomas Aquinas' *Summa Theologiae* (c.
1265–1274), which contains several thousand articles all built to the identical five-part
template described below.

The standard modern history is Alex J. Novikoff, *The Medieval Culture of Disputation:
Pedagogy, Practice, and Performance* (University of Pennsylvania Press, 2013).

## The *quaestio* — five parts, in order

### 1. *Utrum* — "whether"

The question is posed as a **strict binary admitting a yes or a no**.

*Utrum Deus sit* — whether God exists. Not "let us consider the question of God's
existence." The binary framing is what makes the rest of the machinery possible: you
cannot state objections to a topic, only to a proposition.

**This step fails more often than any other.** A badly framed *utrum* — one that presents
a false dilemma, or smuggles a premise, or is really two questions — cannot be rescued by
good work in the later steps. If the binary feels forced, the question is not yet ready
for disputation; it is still a matter for the elenchus.

### 2. *Videtur quod* — "it seems that" (the objections)

Arguments **for the position the author will reject**. Conventionally three.

This is the load-bearing inversion. Aquinas opens every article by arguing *against*
himself, and he argues well — the objections in the *Summa* are frequently the best
short statements of the opposing view available anywhere, which is why they are still
quoted by the people who hold those views.

Three requirements:

- **Strongest form, not convenient form.** The objection must be the one an intelligent
  proponent would actually raise, not the one easiest to knock over.
- **In its own terms.** Stated as its holder would state it, without pre-loaded
  qualifications or a tone that signals it is about to lose.
- **Each must be separately answerable.** Three restatements of one objection is one
  objection.

A useful test: if a proponent of the rejected view read your objections, would they say
"yes, that's my argument" — or would they say "that's not what I said"?

### 3. *Sed contra* — "but on the contrary"

A single decisive consideration that **flips the presumption** the objections have built.
In Aquinas this is usually an authority — scripture, Augustine, Aristotle — deployed as a
pivot rather than as a proof.

Its function is structural, not evidential: after three good objections the reader is
leaning one way, and the *sed contra* halts that momentum so the determination can be
heard. In modern use its analogue is the one hard fact, measurement, or constraint that
the objections have not accounted for.

### 4. *Respondeo dicendum* — "I answer that" (the determination)

The author's own position, argued positively.

The characteristic move here — the thing that makes scholastic method more than
structured pro-and-con — is the **distinction**. Aquinas' *respondeo* typically does not
choose between the objections and his thesis. It identifies an ambiguity in a term that
both sides had been using in one sense, splits it into two senses, and shows that the
apparent contradiction was equivocation.

Something is true *in one respect* and false *in another*: *secundum quid*. The dispute
dissolves not because one side had bad evidence but because the question contained two
questions.

**A *respondeo* that merely picks a winner has not used the method.** It has used a
scoring rubric. The determination is the place where you find the distinction, or
establish that there genuinely isn't one and the objections are simply wrong.

### 5. *Ad primum, ad secundum, ad tertium* — the replies

A **separate reply to each objection, addressed in order**.

This is the step that is skipped, and skipping it is what makes an argument feel slippery
even when its conclusion is correct. Having stated three real objections, you owe each one
an individual account of what it got right and precisely where it fails.

The canonical reply pattern: **concede as much as possible, then locate the exact point of
failure.** Aquinas' replies characteristically run "this would be true if X, but X holds
only *secundum quid*…" — granting the objection's force within its proper scope and
denying only its overreach.

**A reply that restates the *respondeo* is not a reply.** If your answer to objection 2 is
"as explained above," objection 2 was never engaged, and a reader who found it persuasive
still does.

## The live disputation: roles and moves

The written *quaestio* is the record of a live oral event with assigned roles:

| Role | Function |
|---|---|
| ***Opponens*** | Advances objections against the thesis. Obliged to attack in earnest. |
| ***Respondens*** | Must answer every objection, on the spot, using the moves below. |
| ***Magister*** | Delivers the *determinatio* — the binding resolution, typically the next day |

The *respondens* could not deflect, change the subject, or answer at length. Each objection
had to be met with **one of three declared moves**:

- ***Concedo*** — I grant it. (And I must then live with it.)
- ***Nego*** — I deny it, and here is the premise I deny.
- ***Distinguo*** — I distinguish: true in this sense, false in that one.

**The requirement to declare which move you are making is the whole discipline.** A
response that does not resolve to *concedo*, *nego*, or *distinguo* is not a response.
This single constraint eliminates the entire family of evasions — the sympathetic
restatement, the pivot to an adjacent point, the "great question, and also…" — because
none of them can be labelled.

The tradition also ran *quaestiones quodlibetales*: twice-yearly public sessions where
anyone present could pose any question at all, and the master had to determine it. The
format's confidence in itself is worth noting.

## Failure modes

| Failure | What it looks like | Why it is fatal |
|---|---|---|
| **Strawman objection** | The rejected view stated in a form nobody holds | The determination beat something no one was arguing. It proves nothing. |
| **Advocacy in objection clothing** | Objections written with a built-in wince; "some might naively claim…" | Same defect, harder to spot, because the structure looks correct |
| **No distinction in the *respondeo*** | Weighs the options and picks the heavier one | A scoring rubric, not a determination. Nothing was dissolved. |
| **Reply by reference** | "As explained above"; a reply that restates the *respondeo* | The objection stands, unengaged. Its holder is unmoved. |
| **False binary in the *utrum*** | Two options where there are five, or one question where there are two | Every subsequent step inherits the error. Unrecoverable. |
| **Determination deferred** | "Both have merit; more data is needed" | The method exists to determine. Refusing is the one unavailable move. |
| **Capitulation to status** | Position abandoned because of who objected, not what they argued | The objection was never adjudicated. Argument was replaced by rank. |

## Where this applies to agent work

Recorded failures, from runs against a fresh agent with no skill loaded:

- **Capitulation.** Challenged with credentials and no new argument, the agent's opening
  words were *"Understood — shared database it is,"* followed by *"not to relitigate the
  decision."* The user's claim — that service-per-database is over-engineering at their
  scale — is checkable and might be true, and it was neither granted nor denied. There is
  no *concedo* and no *nego*. The position simply evaporated.

- **Verdict without the losing case.** Asked to choose a datastore, the agent chose well
  and defended its choice — but the rejected option's case appeared only as
  concession-in-passing (*"that's the only column in DynamoDB's favor here"*). There is no
  *videtur quod*, so a reader cannot audit whether the loser was beaten or merely
  under-represented.

- **Deferral.** Handed two coherent, contradictory investigation reports and asked which
  was right, the agent said *"I don't want to just pick a side here"* and asked for more
  evidence. It had in fact found the distinction — that A could be a contributing cause
  while B was the primary one — and then declined to declare it.

The through-line: agents are good at *reasoning* toward a position and bad at the
**structure** that makes a position auditable and defensible. Notably, in the one baseline
where the output format already carried that structure — an ADR — the agent steelmanned
the rejected option unprompted and at length. The format did the work.

That is what this method supplies: not better judgment, but a shape that forces judgment
to expose itself.

## What this method cannot do

Disputation **presupposes a well-formed question**. It has no facility for discovering
that the *utrum* is confused, that a key term is equivocal before the *respondeo* stage,
or that the whole dispute rests on a premise no one has examined. Run against a bad
question, it produces a rigorous, well-defended, thoroughly auditable answer to the wrong
thing — and its rigor makes that outcome *more* convincing, not less.

Diagnosing a malformed question is the other method's job. See
[socratic-method.md](./socratic-method.md).
